  These are games you play with your partner on the sofa before bed. Keep
  everything short enough to read at a glance and obvious enough to have an
  opinion about instantly.

  The game gives the SETUP. The players deliver the punchline. If a line is
  already funny on its own, it has done the players' job for them and there is
  nothing left to argue about.

  Shortlist items: 2-4 words. A bare thing. No commas, no "that", no "which".
  All items in a theme should be the same shape (all nouns, or all gerunds) so
  they can be compared at a glance — ranking seven items is 21 separate
  comparisons, so every extra word costs.

      no    the first coffee, before anyone speaks to them
      no    the shower that fogs up the whole flat
      yes   coffee
      yes   long showers

  One modifier at most, never two. The moment you add a clause explaining WHY
  something is funny, you have written the joke instead of the players.

  Never write the detail that proves you know them. Write the flat thing
  they'll fight over. Warmth comes from players defending themselves, not from
  the writer being tender on their behalf.

  Banned: em-dash asides, "specifically", "the good one", "non-negotiable".
  That's the writer winking at the reader.

  Spice: the mix is deliberate. Most entries are safe, some are flirty, a few
  are genuinely rude. Keep that spread when adding more.

  live together, own a house, or have a pet.

# Their Word

The daily puzzle. One of these comes up each day — the same one for both of you.
You each answer it in five or six letters, then solve the other's as a Wordle,
with the question as the only clue.

"{name}" is your partner — the person who'll solve it — so "The animal {name}
reminds you of" is about them, and they find out how you see them.

The best ones are open: there's no obvious answer, so a stranger would never get
it but the person who knows you might. Avoid the ones with only a few answers
(a coffee order, a favourite season), since they're a coin toss, not a test of
how well you know each other. Before adding one, check that a few of the answers
people would really give are five or six letters. Places and names never are,
because they're not in the word list. Keep them short. No "right now", no
"if you could", nothing sentimental.

# Their Numbers

The daily numbers. Five of these come up each day, the same five for both of you.
You each answer them about yourself with a whole number, then guess the other's.
Exactly right is the prize; close counts for something.

Every answer has to be a whole number someone can give without looking anything
up, and it should be about you, not the two of you. Say what the number counts
("hours", "out of 10") so nobody's guessing the units. Keep away from weight,
money and anything with a right answer to be embarrassed about.
